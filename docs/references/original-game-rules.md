# Greed (Farkle / 10,000) - Complete Rulebook

Greed, most commonly known as Farkle or 10,000, is a classic "push-your-luck" dice game. The perfect blend of chance and strategy, it rewards players who know when to bank their points and punishes those who get too greedy.

## 1. Setup & Objective
* **Players:** 2 or more (best with 3-8).
* **Components:** 6 standard six-sided dice, a pen, and a piece of paper for keeping score.
* **Objective:** Be the first player to reach or exceed **10,000 points**. 

## 2. Gameplay Loop
To determine who goes first, each player rolls a single die. The highest roll starts, and play continues clockwise.

### Taking a Turn
1. **The Initial Roll:** On your turn, place all 6 dice in a cup (or your hands) and roll them.
2. **Setting Aside Dice:** After every roll, you **must** set aside at least one "scoring die" or "scoring combination" (see *Scoring* below). 
3. **The "Greed" Decision:** Once you have set aside your scoring dice, calculate your running total for the turn. You now face a choice:
   * **Stop & Bank:** End your turn, record your accumulated points on the scorepad, and pass the remaining dice to the next player.
   * **Push Your Luck (Roll Again):** Leave the scoring dice on the table and roll the *remaining* dice to try and earn more points.
4. **Busting (Farkle!):** If you roll the remaining dice and **no scoring dice appear**, you have "Farkled" (or busted). Your turn ends immediately, you lose *all* points accumulated during that turn, and you pass the dice.

### Hot Dice
If you manage to set aside all 6 dice as scoring dice (either all at once or over several rolls in the same turn), you have "Hot Dice". You may now pick up all 6 dice and roll them again, adding to your current running total for that turn. There is no limit to how many times you can get Hot Dice in a single turn.

## 3. Standard Scoring System
*Note: Scoring combinations only count if made within a single roll. You cannot combine a 1 from your first roll with two 1s from your second roll to make a Three-of-a-Kind.*

| Dice Rolled | Point Value |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Single 5** | 50 points |
| **Single 1** | 100 points |
| **Three 2s** | 200 points |
| **Three 3s** | 300 points |
| **Three 4s** | 400 points |
| **Three 5s** | 500 points |
| **Three 6s** | 600 points |
| **Three 1s** | 1,000 points |
| **Four of a Kind** | 1,000 points |
| **Five of a Kind** | 2,000 points |
| **Six of a Kind** | 3,000 points |

| **Three Pairs** (e.g., 2-2, 4-4, 6-6) | 1,500 points |
| **Two Triplets** (e.g., 2-2-2 and 5-5-5)| 2,500 points |

## 4. Special Rules
* **Getting on the Board:** To start banking points in the game, a player must score at least **500 points** (some play with 1,000 points) in a single turn. Until they hit this threshold, they must keep rolling or bust. Once they are "on the board," they can bank any amount on future turns.
* **The Endgame / Final Round:** The game does not end the exact moment a player hits 10,000 points. Once a player crosses the 10,000-point threshold, they bank their points. Every other player then gets **one final turn** to try and beat that high score. The player with the highest total after this final round wins.

## 5. Winning Strategy
While the dice are random, winning consistently requires strong risk management:

* **The 3-Dice Rule:** Rolling 3 dice is mathematically the tipping point. The odds of busting (rolling no 1s, 5s, or combos) on 3 dice are roughly 27.7%. If you only have 1 or 2 dice left to roll, the odds of busting are very high (44% and 66%, respectively). **General rule:** Bank your points if you only have 1 or 2 non-scoring dice left.
* **Don't Get Attached to 5s:** A single 5 is only worth 50 points, but it eats up a die you could use on your next roll. If you roll a 1 and a 5 alongside four non-scoring dice, it is often strategically wiser to keep the 100 points (the 1) and re-roll five dice instead of four.
* **Scoreboard Awareness:** If you are in the lead, play conservatively and bank steady points. If you are trailing significantly, especially in the late game, you *must* take risks and chase "Hot Dice" to catch up. 
* **The Final Round Push:** If someone triggers the endgame, you have nothing to lose. You must keep rolling until you either beat their score or bust.
